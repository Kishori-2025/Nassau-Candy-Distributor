Executive Summary

Factory Reallocation & Shipping Optimization · Nassau Candy Distributor · 05 November 2026

Purpose

Nassau Candy decides which factory makes each product using fixed, historical rules. This project built a tool that tests other factory assignments before any change is made. It estimates the effect on delivery time, risk and profit, and recommends which changes are worth trying.

What we found

0.57%Lead Time Reduction (operational gain)
100%Profit Impact Stability (financial safety)
38/100Scenario Confidence Score (reliability)
87%Recommendation Coverage (scalability)
  • We analysed 10,122 order lines worth $134,654 in sales across the US and Canada.
  • Moving products between factories would shorten delivery times only slightly, by about 0.6% on average for the 13 products where a move helps.
  • Order timing matters far more than factory location: average delivery time differs by 384 days between order years, while distance shows almost no link.
  • Most suggested moves point to The Other Factory, the most central site. Its capacity to take on more production is unknown.
  • Only 5 of the recommended moves meet our confidence bar. They are all high-volume products with plenty of order history.

Recommendations

  1. Approve a small, measured pilot rather than a network-wide change, starting with one of: Wonka Bar -Scrumdiddlyumptious, Wonka Bar - Nutty Crunch Surprise, Wonka Bar - Fudge Mallows.
  2. Confirm factory readiness (equipment, capacity, food-safety approval) before moving any product line.
  3. Invest in better data: real dispatch and delivery dates, freight costs and factory capacity. This will make future recommendations much more reliable.
  4. Use the dashboard for regular reviews as new data arrives.

Risks and safeguards

  • Some moves would send products to factories that do not make them today. The tool flags these as high-risk.
  • Profit estimates rely on an assumed freight rate, because no shipping-cost data was provided.
  • Recorded delivery times in the data look unrealistic (years rather than days), so results should guide where to look, not replace a real-world trial.
Decision requested: approve a one-product, one-region pilot and the data improvements above. Revisit the full reallocation once pilot results are available.
